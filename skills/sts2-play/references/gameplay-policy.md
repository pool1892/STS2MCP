# STS2 Gameplay Policy

Use this reference when making strategic and tactical choices during a run.

## General Principles

- HP is a resource, but avoid preventable chip damage when it does not buy
  tempo, relic value, or lethal setup.
- Deck quality beats deck size. Skip cards that do not solve a current problem
  or strengthen a clear plan.
- Front-loaded damage is the Act 1 baseline; Act 2 needs enough block, draw,
  debuff, and scaling to survive larger turns.
- Read intents before playing cards. Debuff or buff turns are damage windows.
- Do not block when no enemy attacks unless block has an extra payoff.
- Use potions before dying with them. Potions are especially valuable on elite,
  boss, and large multi-enemy turns.

## Combat Sequencing

- Play zero-cost setup and draw first unless draw prevention makes that bad.
- Apply Vulnerable and Weak before attacks when possible.
- Use skills before attacks when enemy mechanics reward card count or when draw
  can change the attack line.
- Use biggest attacks after debuffs and damage multipliers are active.
- If lethal is certain, skip block and finish the fight.
- Against multi-hit or thorns-sensitive enemies, count hit number as well as
  damage.
- Against leader/minion fights, target the leader when minions flee or become
  irrelevant after leader death.

## Ironclad Heuristics From This Run

- Perfected Strike packages value every Strike-name card and improve with
  Pommel Strike, Twin Strike, and similar cards.
- Draw-on-play effects are strong with Hellraiser because drawn Strike-name
  cards can become damage.
- Stampede rewards leaving a useful attack in hand at end turn when the free
  play is likely valuable.
- Flame Barrier is premium when enemies multi-hit or attack into thorns.
- Shrug It Off style block plus draw is valuable because it defends without
  freezing the deck.
- Offering is a high-value burst card but spend its HP only when the current
  hand can convert the energy and draw.

## Map And Rest Decisions

- Take elites when HP and deck quality can support them, especially with combat
  potions or a rest soon after.
- After a profitable elite, reassess instead of carrying risk momentum forward.
- Rest before boss or dangerous Act 2 paths when HP is low enough that a bad
  draw can end the run.
- Smith when HP is stable and an upgrade improves the main plan or blocks a
  known upcoming threat.
- Shops are better with enough gold to buy a card/relic/removal that solves a
  current weakness.

## Card Reward Decisions

Prefer cards that improve one of these:

- immediate damage
- reliable block
- draw or energy conversion
- AoE for multi-enemy fights
- Vulnerable, Weak, or other fight-shortening debuff
- scaling for elites and bosses
- synergy with the current deck's actual observed engine

Skip when the offer only adds unfocused damage, redundant support, or expensive
cards that do not swing a fight.

## Event Decisions

- Prefer permanent upside with manageable downside.
- Avoid curses unless the upside is unusually large or removal is nearby.
- Card enchantments should go on central engine or payoff cards, not merely on
  the highest standalone card.
- Removal is strongest on starter Strikes/Defends when the deck no longer needs
  them for a payoff package. If Perfected Strike is central, removing Strikes is
  less attractive.

## Current-Run Lessons To Preserve

- Vantom's Slippery makes cheap hit count valuable before big single-hit
  attacks.
- Perfect Fit on a core payoff card can improve redraw consistency after
  shuffles.
- Act 2 at low HP should favor rest and survival over optional elite greed.
- Bowlbug-style multi-enemy fights punish slow setup; stabilize or kill one
  target quickly to reduce incoming damage.
